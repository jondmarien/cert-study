## Core idea

Security+ groups the exam into five domains. Published SY0-701 weights put general concepts near 12 percent, threats and mitigations near 22, architecture near 18, operations near 28, and program management near 20. Confirm those numbers on CompTIA's current outline. Weights tell you where to spend drill time with your coach. They do not change what a term means.

This domain is the shared vocabulary:

- What you are trying to protect, which the foundations note covers as confidentiality, integrity, availability, and the ideas that sit beside them
- How a safeguard is categorized, which the controls note covers
- How identity is proved and what it may do, which shows up again under operations
- Why "trust the network location" is out of fashion, which the zero trust note covers
- How cryptography supports the goals, without turning you into a cryptographer

Later domains apply this vocabulary to attackers, networks, day-to-day work, and the program that decides risk. If a practice item feels like a scenario, translate it back into a goal and a control type before you pick an answer. That translation is the skill.

<Exam>

Items in this domain usually ask you to name the idea, not to design a company. When two answers are both "security things," the difference is the goal (secrecy versus uptime versus proof) or the control type (stop it, notice it, reduce the damage, undo it).

</Exam>

<Pitfall>

Memorizing the five domain titles and skipping the terms inside them. Treating the published percentages as permanent. Using BSCP lab detail as the definition of a Security+ term. The lab note is a web example. The exam wants the category.

</Pitfall>
